The short answer: the closest real hot springs to Las Vegas are Gold Strike and Arizona Hot Spring. Both sit in Black Canyon below Hoover Dam, about 45 minutes from the Strip. Both are hike-in only. Both close every summer from May 15 to September 30.

If you want to drive up and soak without a hike, go to Tecopa, California, about 90 minutes west. Or go to Zion Canyon Hot Springs in Utah, about two hours north.

Details below were checked in October 2026.

## Black Canyon: The Closest Soaks

Black Canyon is the stretch of the Colorado River just below Hoover Dam. It sits inside Lake Mead National Recreation Area. Several hot springs seep out of the canyon walls here.

**Fees.** Lake Mead charges $25 per private vehicle for 1 to 7 days. It is $15 per person on foot or bike, and $20 per motorcycle. Kids under 16 are free. A Lake Mead annual pass is $45. The park is cashless, so bring a card. America the Beautiful passes work too.

**Summer closure.** The Gold Strike and Arizona Hot Spring trails close May 15 through September 30 every year. Canyon temperatures can pass 115°F. People have died on these hikes in summer heat. The closure is enforced.

### Gold Strike Hot Springs, Nevada

Gold Strike is the closest hot spring to Las Vegas. The hike is about 5.5 miles round trip with about 1,000 feet of elevation change. You drop down a boulder-choked canyon with eight fixed rope sections, each around 20 feet. Expect Class 3 scrambling.

The ropes are put there by past visitors and volunteers. The park does not maintain or inspect them. Test each one before you trust it.

Pools run about 100°F up high and about 109°F near the source. Plan on 6 to 8 hours round trip with soaking time. The hike out is harder than the hike in.

**Getting there:** Take US 93 toward the Hoover Dam bridge. Exit at Exit 2, turn right, then left onto a short dirt road to a free lot. The address is Goldstrike Pass Road, Boulder City, NV 89005.

### Arizona Hot Spring (Ringbolt), Arizona

Arizona Hot Spring is just across the river on the Arizona side. The hike is about 5 to 6 miles round trip with roughly 750 feet of elevation change. Near the spring you climb a 20-foot ladder up a slot canyon.

Source water comes out around 111 to 120°F. The water steps down the canyon through a series of pools. Each one is a little cooler than the one above, so you can pick your heat.

**Getting there:** Park off US 93 on the Arizona side, before mile marker 4, south of Hoover Dam.

### Boy Scout Hot Springs, Arizona

Boy Scout is another Black Canyon spring. It is harder to reach. You need a technical backcountry hike or a kayak trip from below Hoover Dam. If you paddle Black Canyon, you can link several springs in one trip.

## Drive-Up Soaks Worth the Trip

### Tecopa, California

Tecopa is a tiny desert town on the edge of Death Valley. It is about 70 to 80 miles west of Las Vegas, roughly 90 minutes. It has the closest drive-up mineral soaks to the city.

**Tecopa Hot Springs Resort** sells a $15 day soak. That covers the indoor mineral tubs and outdoor mud baths. Pools run 102 to 108°F. The soaks are clothing optional, and family windows are available. Mineral water can stain swimsuits.

**Delight's Hot Springs Resort** sells day passes too. It was last reported at $35 per person. Call ahead for current pricing.

Shoshone is a few miles away if you want food or gas. Visit October to May. Summer here is brutal.

### Zion Canyon Hot Springs, Utah

This is the polished option. It is about 138 miles and two hours north on I-15 in La Verkin, Utah. Utah is one hour ahead of Nevada, so watch your booking time.

Adult passes start at $46 off-peak. Kids 3 to 12 are $25. The 21+ side starts at $57 off-peak. Peak days cost more. You get more than 30 geothermal pools, saunas and cold plunges. Bring ID for the 21+ side.

## Spots That Disappoint

**Rogers Spring** on Northshore Road in Lake Mead is a pretty warm pond, around 80 to 90°F. The Park Service does not recommend swimming there. Go for the palm trees and the fish, not a soak.

**Blue Point Spring** nearby runs 84 to 90°F but is too shallow to soak in.

**Tule Springs** in north Las Vegas is a fossil beds site, not a hot spring.

## Going Further

- **Fish Lake Valley Hot Well** near Dyer has a long concrete tub around 105°F. It is open year-round on a rough dirt road. [Nevada hot springs map](/blog/nevada-hot-springs-map)

## Tips for Any Black Canyon Soak

- **Go October through April.** May 14 is the last day before the trails close.
- **Start early.** Even in spring, the climb out gets hot by midday.
- **Carry more water than you think.** Hot water dehydrates you faster.
- **Keep your head above water.** Desert hot springs can carry Naegleria fowleri, a rare but deadly amoeba. Do not dunk your head or splash water in your nose.
- **Pack out everything.** These canyons have no trash service.

The short answer: a paid Utah hot spring costs $18 to $46 or more per person and buys you a reserved, maintained pool. A free one costs nothing at the gate but trades that for a hike, a dirt road, or a muddy bottom. Neither is better. They are different trips.

Below is every major soak in the state, sorted by what you pay. Prices were checked in October 2026. They change often, so confirm before you drive.

## Quick Comparison

| Spring | Cost | Reservation | Access | Water temp |

## The Paid Springs

### Crystal Hot Springs, Honeyville

Crystal is the cheapest paid soak in Utah at $18 for day use. Water slides cost $2 more. Kids 3 and under get in free. It sits about an hour north of Salt Lake City.

You get a big soaking pool, three hot tubs, a freshwater pool, a lap pool and two slides. Source water comes out at 134°F, and the pools are cooled to safe soaking temps. This is the pick for families and anyone who wants a full afternoon. Slide hours shrink in winter, so check the daily schedule.

Read more: [Crystal Hot Springs guide](/blog/crystal-hot-springs-utah-your-ultimate-guide-to-soaking-in-the-desert-s-warmest-waters)

### Mystic Hot Springs, Monroe

Mystic is famous for its clawfoot bathtubs set into a travertine hillside. A pass costs $28.41 for adults and $22.73 for kids 12 and under. That buys a reserved two-hour slot. Mystic is reservation only, so do not show up without one.

Tubs and pools run 99 to 110°F. You share the soaking area with everyone else in your time slot. Bring your own towel and a refillable water bottle. Camping and pioneer cabins sit right outside if you want to stay over.

Read more: [Mystic Hot Springs guide](/blog/mystic-hot-springs-utah-the-ultimate-desert-soaking-guide) and [Crystal vs Mystic](/blog/crystal-vs-mystic-hot-springs)

### Homestead Crater, Midway

This one is a 65-foot-deep geothermal pool inside a limestone dome. It costs $30 for a one-hour session. You get about 40 minutes in the water. Life vests are required and included.

The water stays 90 to 96°F all year. That is warm, not hot. People come for the setting more than the heat. Soak hours run 10:30 a.m. to 9:30 p.m. on weekdays and 9:30 a.m. to 9:30 p.m. on weekends. Reserve ahead for weekends and holidays. Walk-ins only get in if there is space.

One planning note: the Crater is scheduled to close for renovations in April and May 2027.

Read more: [Homestead Crater guide](/blog/homestead-crater-utah-a-complete-guide-to-the-desert-southwest-s-most-unique-hot-spring)

### Zion Canyon Hot Springs, La Verkin

This is the most expensive soak in Utah and the most developed. Adult passes start at $46 off-peak. Kids 3 to 12 are $25. The 21+ Premier Access side starts at $57 off-peak. Peak pricing runs much higher.

For that you get more than 30 geothermal pools, barrel saunas, cold plunges, a snack bar and private cabanas. The water comes from the historic Pah Tempe spring, which is no longer open for public soaking on its own. It sits about 20 miles from Zion National Park.

Read more: [Hot springs near Zion](/blog/hot-springs-near-zion-national-park)

### Two Paid Spots to Know About

**Veyo Pool** costs $18 (under 2 free) and is open April 1 to October 31. It is a warm spring-fed swimming pool, not a hot soak. Go for swimming, not for heat. [Veyo Pool guide](/blog/veyo-pool-utah-your-complete-guide-to-the-desert-southwest-s-hidden-thermal-oasis)

**Belmont Hot Springs** near Fielding posts hours of 9 a.m. to 10 p.m., but day-use access is not guaranteed. Call before you go.

## The Free Springs

### Saratoga Hot Springs (Inlet Park), Saratoga Springs

This is the easiest free soak in Utah. It is about 40 minutes south of Salt Lake City inside a city park. A short path leads from the parking lot to three pools. The hottest reaches about 109°F. The pool nearest the lake is cooler and muddier.

The bottoms are mud, so bring water shoes. Swimsuits are required. No alcohol. The park closes at 10 p.m. Restrooms close for the season from October 15 to April 15.

Read more: [Saratoga Hot Springs guide](/blog/saratoga-hot-springs-utah-the-ultimate-desert-soaking-guide-for-2025)

### Fifth Water Hot Springs, Diamond Fork Canyon

Fifth Water is Utah's best known hike-in soak. The soak is free, but parking needs a $10 Forest Service vehicle pass good for three days. The hike is about 4.5 to 5 miles round trip when the road is open. In winter the road closes and the hike grows to about 8 miles.

The trail is steep in spots and passes three waterfalls. Lower pools are hotter and busier. Upper pools are cooler and quieter. Expect a strong sulfur smell. Nudity is illegal here.

The Forest Service warns that harmful bacteria may be present. Do not swallow the water or put your head under. Arrive early on weekends.

Read more: [Fifth Water guide](/blog/fifth-water-hot-springs-utah-s-ultimate-desert-soaking-adventure)

### Meadow Hot Springs, Meadow

Meadow sits in a farmer's field off I-15, about 1.5 hours south of Provo. It is on private land. The owner allows visitors and built a trail and fence. Treat it that way. Pack out everything and follow posted rules.

The walk from parking is about half a mile. The main pool averages about 100°F and is deep enough for scuba divers to reach an underwater cave. Two smaller pools are cooler and shallower. Swimsuits are required. No alcohol. The dirt road gets muddy after rain.

Read more: [Meadow Hot Springs guide](/blog/meadow-hot-springs-utah-your-complete-guide-to-soaking-in-utah-s-hidden-desert-gem)

### Baker Hot Springs, near Delta

Baker is three concrete tubs in the West Desert, about two hours southwest of Provo. The source water is very hot. Cold spring water runs in through pipes, and you mix it yourself to get a safe temperature. Test before you get in.

Each tub fits a few people. There are no services. The dirt approach can become impassable after storms.

### Gandy Warm Springs, near the Nevada line

Gandy is remote and warm rather than hot. There is no admission and no development. Rain or snow can change the dirt roads fast, so check the forecast and carry extra water.

Read more: [Gandy Warm Springs guide](/blog/gandy-warm-springs-utah-the-complete-desert-oasis-guide-for-soakers)

### Red Hill Hot Springs, Monroe

Red Hill is also in Monroe, close to Mystic. There is no posted fee. Access is informal and can change. If signs go up or the property status changes, respect them and leave.

## Springs You Cannot Soak Right Now

- **Ogden Hot Pots** are on private property with active no-trespassing enforcement. No reopening date has been announced.
- **Pah Tempe** in La Verkin is closed to public soaking. Its water now feeds Zion Canyon Hot Springs.
- **Horseshoe Springs** is a wildlife management area, not a soaking spot.

## How to Choose

**Pick a paid spring if** you have kids, want a restroom and a locker, need a guaranteed spot, or are visiting in winter when free spring roads get rough. Crystal is the best value. Homestead is the most unusual. Mystic has the best views.

**Pick a free spring if** you want fewer rules, do not mind mud or a hike, and can be flexible on timing. Saratoga is the easiest. Fifth Water is the best day hike. Meadow and Baker are the best road trip stops.

**Either way,** check the details the day before. Paid springs change prices and hours. Free springs change access when roads wash out or landowners change their minds.

## What to Bring to the Hot Springs

A well-packed bag makes the difference between a relaxing soak and a shivering dash back to the car. Here's what experienced soakers never leave home without.

### Essentials (Don't Forget)

**Quick-dry towel** — Cotton towels stay wet and heavy. A microfiber or quick-dry towel packs small, dries fast, and won't soak your car seat on the drive home.

**Swimsuit** — Even at clothing-optional springs, bring one. You'll want it for changing areas, nearby hikes, or conservative neighbors.

**Stainless steel water bottle** — Soaking dehydrates you faster than you realize. Bring plenty of water and drink before, during, and after your soak.

**Sandals or water shoes** — Rocky paths, hot surfaces, and slippery pool decks are standard. Protect your feet with something that can get wet.

**Mineral sunscreen** — High-altitude springs mean intense UV. Apply before you go and reapply after soaking.

### Nice to Have

**Dry bag** — Keeps your phone, keys, and clothes dry at primitive springs where there's no locker or changing room.

**Insulated cooler bag** — Bring cold drinks and snacks. A post-soak beer or cold water hits different.

**Headlamp** — Essential for night visits to primitive springs. Hot spring water + darkness + rocky trails = twisted ankles without one.

**First aid kit** — Remote springs mean remote help. Bandages, tweezers (for splinters), and blister pads go a long way.

**Phone dry pouch** — If you want photos at the springs, a waterproof pouch keeps your phone alive. Cheap insurance for a $1000 device.

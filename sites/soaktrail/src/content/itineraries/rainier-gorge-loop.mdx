## Trip Overview

The Rainier-to-Gorge Loop is the ideal Washington hot springs weekend: three distinct soaking experiences across the state's most scenic landscapes, all connected by a 280-mile loop that never backtracks.

You'll start in the old-growth forests of Mount Rainier National Park, wind through the Cascade foothills via the White Pass Scenic Byway, soak in the historic mineral baths of the Columbia Gorge, and finish with a rustic alpine plunge in the shadow of Mount Baker.

## What Makes This Trip Special

- **Three ecosystems in three days**: temperate rainforest, dry Gorge pine country, and subalpine Cascades
- **Variety of soak types**: primitive wilderness pools, a developed historic resort, and a rustic trail-side spring
- **No ferry required**: all stops are accessible by standard vehicle on paved roads
- **Flexible lodging**: mix of camping and small-town lodging lets you tailor the trip to your budget

## Day-by-Day Highlights

### Ohanapecosh Hot Springs

Tucked inside Mount Rainier National Park, Ohanapecosh is a primitive spring in a stunning old-growth setting. The pools are small and natural, with temperatures varying by season. The short walk from the Ohanapecosh Campground passes through towering Douglas firs and cedars, some over 500 years old.

> **Tip**: Ohanapecosh is free to visit (park entrance fee required), but the pools can be cool in shoulder seasons. Mid-summer is the sweet spot when snow melt has subsided and the water is at its warmest.

### Carson Hot Springs

Carson Hot Springs is a historic resort in the Columbia Gorge that has been welcoming soakers since the 1890s. The stone soaking tubs are fed by natural mineral springs and the property offers lodging, making it a comfortable mid-trip base. The Gorge views from the property are spectacular, especially at sunset.

### Baker Hot Springs

Also known as Baker River Hot Springs, this rustic site requires a short hike but rewards with views of Mount Baker and the surrounding wilderness. The pools are natural and unmaintained, so conditions vary. It's a fitting finale to the loop: one last soak before the drive home.

## Planning Notes

- **Mount Rainier entrance fee**: $30 per vehicle (7-day pass) or use your America the Beautiful pass
- **Carson Hot Springs**: reservations recommended for overnight stays, especially summer weekends
- **Baker Hot Springs**: check road conditions before visiting; the access road can be rough after storms
- **Fuel**: fill up in Stevenson or Carson before heading to Baker; services are sparse on the North Cascades leg

## Trip Overview

The Desert Canyon Soak Loop showcases the surreal beauty of desert hot springs, where steaming mineral water emerges from arid landscapes. You'll soak in natural limestone pools, hike to waterfall-fed springs in red rock canyons, and even soak inside a 10,000-year-old limestone crater.

This trip is best in cooler months — the desert can be brutally hot in summer, and soaking in 100°F water when it's 110°F outside isn't pleasant. October through April is the sweet spot, when the contrast between cool desert air and hot mineral water is perfect.

## What Makes This Trip Special

- **Limestone crater soak**: Homestead Crater is a one-of-a-kind experience — a 55-foot dome over a 90°F spring
- **Waterfall springs**: Fifth Water's milky-blue pools have a cascading waterfall
- **Luxury and primitive**: Zion Canyon Hot Springs offers 50+ pools, while Meadow is free and natural
- **Red rock scenery**: drive through some of Utah's most dramatic landscapes
- **Year-round access**: all stops are on paved roads

## Planning Notes

- **Avoid summer**: desert temps 100°F+ make soaking uncomfortable; visit Oct-Apr
- **Fifth Water hike**: 2.5 miles each way, $10 parking fee at trailhead
- **Homestead Crater**: reservations required for soaking, $16 for 90 minutes
- **Zion Canyon Hot Springs**: newly opened (2026), $39+ entry, family and adult sections
- **Meadow Hot Springs**: free, on private property — respect the land and pack out trash

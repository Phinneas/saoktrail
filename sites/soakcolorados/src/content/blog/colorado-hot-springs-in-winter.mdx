The short answer: winter is a great time to soak in Colorado, as long as you pick a developed spring. Glenwood, Iron Mountain, Ouray, Mount Princeton, Pagosa Springs, Strawberry Park and Hot Sulphur Springs all stay open through the season. The big backcountry soaks, like Conundrum and Rainbow, are snowbound and in avalanche terrain. Skip them unless you are a trained winter traveler.

Getting there is the harder part. From September 1 to May 31, Colorado's passenger vehicle traction law applies on I-70. Strawberry Park goes further and requires 4WD with snow tires from November 1 to May 1.

## Springs That Work in Winter

These are developed springs with plowed parking and staff. They are open year-round, though hours and features can change by season.

| Spring | Town | Winter notes |
|---|---|---|
| Glenwood Hot Springs Pool | Glenwood Springs | Big pool, easy access off I-70, lodging on site |
| Iron Mountain Hot Springs | Glenwood Springs | Small pools along the Colorado River, reservations encouraged |
| Ouray Hot Springs | Ouray | Town pool in a snowy box canyon, some features seasonal |
| Mount Princeton Hot Springs | Nathrop | Creekside soaking under the Collegiate Peaks |
| The Springs Resort | Pagosa Springs | Terraced pools along the San Juan River |
| Strawberry Park Hot Springs | Steamboat Springs | Rock pools in the snow, strict winter vehicle rules |
| Hot Sulphur Springs Resort | Hot Sulphur Springs | Many small pools, lodging on site |
| Avalanche Ranch | Redstone | Smaller resort near the Crystal River, book ahead |
| Cottonwood Hot Springs | Buena Vista | Quiet inn with cabin tubs outside town |

### Glenwood Springs

Glenwood is the easiest winter soak from Denver. You stay on I-70 the whole way, and both resorts sit right by the highway. Glenwood Hot Springs Pool is built around one huge main pool. [Iron Mountain](/blog/iron-mountain-hot-springs) has many smaller pools at different temperatures. Steam rolls off both on cold mornings.

### Strawberry Park, Steamboat Springs

[Strawberry Park](/blog/strawberry-hot-springs-colorado) is the most dramatic winter soak in the state. It is also the strictest to reach. From November 1 to May 1, the spring requires a 4WD vehicle with snow tires. Two-wheel drive cars are not allowed, even with chains. The last two miles are steep, winding dirt.

If you do not have the right vehicle, book a shuttle from town. A few other rules to know:

- Admission is cash only.
- No children after dark.
- No Uber or Lyft drop-offs.
- Parking is very limited.

### Ouray

[Ouray Hot Springs](/blog/ouray-hot-springs-pool) is a city-run pool in a deep mountain bowl. In winter the town is busy with ice climbers. The easier drive is US 550 from Montrose to the north. The road south of town crosses Red Mountain Pass, which is prone to closures in storms.

### Pagosa Springs

[The Springs Resort](/blog/the-springs-resort-pagosa-springs) has dozens of pools stepped down to the San Juan River. Coming from the east means crossing Wolf Creek Pass, which gets heavy snow all winter. Check the forecast before you commit.

### Chaffee County: Mount Princeton and Cottonwood

The Buena Vista and Salida area is a good winter pick for Front Range drivers who want to skip I-70. Mount Princeton sits on Chalk Creek below the peaks. [Cottonwood](/blog/cottonwood-hot-springs) is smaller and quieter. See also our [Salida hot springs guide](/blog/salida-hot-springs-visitor-guide).

### One Free Option: Penny Hot Springs

[Penny Hot Springs](/blog/penny-hot-springs-colorado) sits right off Highway 133 between Carbondale and Redstone. There is no hike, so it works in winter. Late summer through winter is its best window, because spring runoff floods the pools. Expect icy rocks, no facilities and a busy pullout on weekends.

## Springs to Skip in Winter

- **Conundrum Hot Springs.** From early October to late June, the trailhead, trail and campsites near Aspen are snow-covered and in avalanche terrain. The route climbs to about 11,400 feet. This is a trip for experienced backcountry skiers with avalanche training and gear, not a casual soak. [Conundrum guide](/blog/colorados-conundrum-hot-springs)
- **Rainbow Hot Springs.** The realistic season in the Weminuche Wilderness is mid-July to early October. [Rainbow guide](/blog/rainbow-hot-springs)
- **Piedra River Hot Springs.** The Forest Service access road usually closes from late fall through April.
- **Radium Hot Springs.** It is reachable in winter, but the last stretch is a steep rock chimney. Snow and ice make it risky. [Radium guide](/blog/radium-hot-springs-colorado)

## Winter Road Rules

**The traction law.** From September 1 through May 31, every passenger vehicle on I-70 between Dotsero and Morrison needs at least 3/16 inch of tread and one of these:

- 4WD or AWD
- Tires with the M+S (mud and snow) mark
- Winter tires with the mountain-snowflake icon
- Tires with a manufacturer all-weather rating
- Chains or an approved traction device

A violation costs $100 plus a $32 surcharge. If your car blocks a lane, it jumps to $500 plus a $156 surcharge.

**Check conditions before you leave.** Colorado's official road site is [COtrip.org](https://www.cotrip.org). It shows closures, chain law status and pass cameras. Mountain passes can close with little warning.

**Give yourself daylight.** Many springs sit at the end of a canyon road. Driving out after an evening soak means dark, icy curves.

## What to Bring for a Winter Soak

Our [general packing list](/blog/what-to-bring-colorado-hot-springs) covers the basics. Winter adds a few things:

- **Slip-on boots or warm sandals** for the walk from the locker room or car.
- **A warm hat.** Your head stays out of the water. A wet head in single digits gets cold fast.
- **A robe or a big parka** to throw on between pools.
- **Two towels.** One to dry off, one to stand on.
- **A dry bag** for your wet suit on the ride home.
- **Water.** Hot water plus altitude dehydrates you faster than you expect.

## Winter Safety

- **Watch for ice.** Decks, steps and parking lots freeze. Walk slowly.
- **Limit your time.** Hot water at altitude can make you light-headed. Take breaks and get out if you feel dizzy.
- **Plan the drive back first.** Do not let a late soak turn into a night drive over a pass in a storm.

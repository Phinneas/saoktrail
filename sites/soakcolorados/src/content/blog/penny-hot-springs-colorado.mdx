<h2 id="is-it-safe-penny-hot-springs-safety-guide">Is It Safe? Penny Hot Springs Safety Guide</h2>

<div style="background: #fef3c7; border-left: 4px solid #f59e0b; padding: 16px; margin: 20px 0; border-radius: 4px;">
<strong>⚠️ Important Safety Information</strong> — Penny Hot Springs is a natural, undeveloped site on BLM land. There are no lifeguards, no facilities, and real hazards that visitors should understand.
</div>

<h3>Spring Runoff Flood Risk</h3>
<p><strong>This is the most serious safety concern at Penny Hot Springs.</strong> The Crystal River can rise rapidly during spring snowmelt (typically May through mid-June). The river runs directly alongside and sometimes through the soaking pools. During high water, the current is strong enough to sweep a person off their feet. <strong>Do not soak during or immediately after high water events.</strong> Check the USGS Crystal River near Redstone gauge (USGS station 09072500) before visiting — if flow exceeds approximately 500 CFS, the pools may be dangerous or submerged.</p>

<h3>Parking Reality</h3>
<p>Parking is roadside along Highway 133. There is no designated parking lot. During peak summer weekends, cars line the highway for several hundred yards in both directions. The road is narrow in the Crystal River Gorge — pull completely off the pavement. <strong>Do not block the road or private driveways.</strong> Arrive early (before 10 AM) on summer weekends or expect to park farther away and walk.</p>

<h3>No Facilities — Plan Accordingly</h3>
<p>There are no restrooms, no changing rooms, no trash cans, and no water source at Penny Hot Springs. Bring: water (at least 1 liter per person), snacks, a towel, a first aid kit, and a bag for trash. Cell service is minimal in the Crystal River Gorge — tell someone your plans before you go.</p>

<h3>Seasonal Access Guidance</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Late October–April:</strong> Coldest conditions. River is low, pools are at their warmest relative to air temp. Bring warm layers for the walk from the road. Ice may be present on rocks.</li>
<li><strong>May–mid-June (Runoff):</strong> Highest risk period. River rises significantly. Pools may be partially or fully submerged. Check USGS flow data before visiting. <strong>If in doubt, don't soak.</strong></li>
<li><strong>Late June–September:</strong> Best conditions. River is lower, pools are clear, water temps are comfortable. This is peak season — expect crowds on weekends.</li>
</ul>

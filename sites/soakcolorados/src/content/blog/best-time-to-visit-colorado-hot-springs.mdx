The short answer: September and October are the best months for most Colorado hot springs. Rivers are low, trails are clear, and the summer crowds are gone. Winter is ideal for developed resorts. Summer is the only real window for backcountry springs. Spring is the toughest season, because snowmelt floods many of the free springs that sit beside rivers.

The right time depends on which kind of spring you want.

## Quick Guide by Season

| Season | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Fall (Sept to Oct) | Almost everything | Early snow at high trailheads |
| Winter (Nov to Mar) | Resorts and town pools | Traction law, icy roads, closed backcountry |
| Spring (Apr to Jun) | Resorts | Flooded and cold river-side springs |
| Summer (Jul to Aug) | Backcountry hikes | Crowds, afternoon storms, hot pools on hot days |

## Fall: The Best All-Around Season

Fall is when the most springs work at once. Spring runoff is long over, so river-side pools like [Penny](/blog/penny-hot-springs-colorado) and [Radium](/blog/radium-hot-springs-colorado) are back to their normal temperature. High trails are still open in September. The air is cool enough that hot water feels good, and aspens turn gold in the mountains.

Backcountry springs close their season in fall. Rainbow Hot Springs runs about mid-July to early October. Conundrum is usually best from July to September. After that, snow and avalanche risk take over.

**Fall picks:** Penny, Radium, Piedra River, Rainbow (early fall), and the Chaffee County resorts around Buena Vista and Salida.

## Winter: Best for Resorts

Steam on a snowy day is the classic Colorado soak. Developed springs like Glenwood, Iron Mountain, Ouray, Pagosa Springs, Mount Princeton and Strawberry Park stay open all winter.

The trade-off is the drive. Colorado's passenger vehicle traction law applies on I-70 from September 1 to May 31. Strawberry Park requires 4WD with snow tires from November 1 to May 1. Backcountry springs are off the table for most people.

Read the full breakdown in our [Colorado hot springs in winter](/blog/colorado-hot-springs-in-winter) guide.

**Winter picks:** Glenwood Springs, Ouray, Pagosa Springs, Strawberry Park, Mount Princeton.

## Spring: The Runoff Problem

Spring is the one season to plan around. As mountain snow melts, rivers rise. Many free springs sit right at the water's edge, and high water floods them with cold river water.

- **Radium** is often flooded and cold from about March through late May.
- **Penny** floods during runoff and goes cold until the river drops.
- **Piedra River** often has its lower pools underwater until July.

Resorts are not affected, so spring is a fine time for a developed soak. Our [spring runoff guide](/blog/spring-runoff-and-colorado-hot-springs-the-timing-guide-that-could-save-your-soa) covers which springs to skip and when.

**Spring picks:** Any resort or town pool. Save the river-side free springs for later.

## Summer: The Backcountry Window

Summer is the only season when Colorado's big wilderness soaks are reachable. Snow clears from the high trails in July. Conundrum near Aspen and Rainbow near Pagosa Springs both peak from mid-July into September. Conundrum needs an overnight permit if you plan to camp.

Summer has downsides too. Afternoon thunderstorms are common in the mountains, so start hikes early. Popular resorts are at their busiest. And a 104°F pool on a 90°F afternoon is less appealing than it sounds. Soak early or late in the day.

**Summer picks:** [Conundrum](/blog/colorados-conundrum-hot-springs), [Rainbow](/blog/rainbow-hot-springs), and evening soaks at the resorts.

## Best Time of Week and Day

- **Weekdays beat weekends.** Free springs with small pullouts, like Penny, fill fast on Saturdays.
- **Go early or late.** Mornings and evenings are quieter and cooler at every spring.
- **Avoid holiday weekends** at the big resorts unless you have a reservation.
- **Leave the backcountry by early afternoon** in summer to beat lightning.

## Best Time by Spring

| Spring | Best window | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Penny | Late summer to winter | Pools flood during spring runoff |
| Radium | Summer to fall | Flooded and cold about March to late May |
| Piedra River | July to early October | High water before July, road closes late fall |
| Rainbow | Mid-July to early October | Wilderness trail is snowbound otherwise |
| Conundrum | July to September | Avalanche terrain from early October to late June |
| Strawberry Park | Year-round | Winter needs 4WD with snow tires |
| Glenwood, Ouray, Pagosa, Mount Princeton | Year-round | Developed and plowed |

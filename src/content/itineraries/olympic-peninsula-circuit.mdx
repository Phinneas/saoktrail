## Trip Overview

The Olympic Peninsula Circuit is a four-day journey through Washington's most diverse landscapes: temperate rainforest, wild Pacific coast, and the San Juan Islands. The centerpiece is a ferry crossing to Orcas Island, where Doe Bay Hot Spring offers one of the most scenic soaks in the Pacific Northwest, perched above the Salish Sea.

This is an easy trip suitable for all fitness levels. The driving is straightforward, the soaks are accessible, and the lodging options range from historic lodges to island resorts.

## What Makes This Trip Special

- **Three distinct Washington ecosystems**: Olympic rainforest, Pacific coast, and San Juan Islands
- **Doe Bay Hot Spring**: one of the most photographed hot springs in the US, with ocean views from the soaking tub
- **Historic lodging**: Sol Duc Resort and Lake Quinault Lodge are both classic Pacific Northwest lodges
- **Ferry adventure**: the Anacortes-Orcas Island ferry is part of the experience, not just transit

## Ferry Planning

The Washington State Ferries Anacortes-Orcas Island route requires advance planning:

- **Reservations strongly recommended**: summer sailings fill up weeks in advance. Book at [wsdot.com/ferries](https://www.wsdot.com/ferries/)
- **Arrive early**: even with a reservation, plan to arrive 30-45 minutes before your sailing
- **Have a backup plan**: if your sailing is canceled due to weather or mechanical issues, the Olympic Peninsula has plenty of hot springs to explore instead
- **Walk-on option**: if you can't get a vehicle reservation, walk-on tickets are usually available (you'd need to arrange island transportation separately)

## Day-by-Day Highlights

### Sol Duc Hot Springs

Sol Duc Hot Springs Resort is nestled in the Sol Duc Valley of Olympic National Park. Three mineral-rich pools at different temperatures sit surrounded by old-growth forest. The resort offers cabin lodging, a restaurant, and access to trails including the popular Sol Duc Falls hike.

### Lake Quinault

The Lake Quinault area is the heart of the temperate rainforest. While not a hot springs destination, the historic Lake Quinault Lodge (built in 1926) is worth the stop for its Roosevelt-era architecture, rainforest trails, and lakeside setting. It's a peaceful overnight between the coast and the ferry.

### Doe Bay Hot Spring

Doe Bay Resort on Orcas Island is the crown jewel of this itinerary. The clothing-optional hot spring sits on a rocky point above the bay, with views of the San Juan Islands and, on clear days, the Olympic Mountains. The resort also offers yoga classes, massage, and organic food at the Doe Bay Cafe.

> **Tip**: Doe Bay is a popular destination and the soaking area is small. Visit early morning or evening for the most peaceful experience. Day passes are available if you're not staying overnight.

## Planning Notes

- **Olympic National Park entrance fee**: $30 per vehicle (7-day pass)
- **Sol Duc Hot Springs**: pool access is fee-based (~$15/person); lodging reservations essential in summer
- **Doe Bay Resort**: soaking is for overnight guests and day-pass holders; book well in advance for summer weekends
- **Ferry reservations**: book at least 2-3 weeks ahead for summer travel
- **Fuel**: gas up in Port Angeles or Forks; services on the west side of the Olympics are limited

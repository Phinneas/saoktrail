## Trip Overview

The Glacier Peak Wilderness Challenge is not for the faint of heart. This three-day backpacking trip takes you deep into the Glacier Peak Wilderness, one of the most rugged and remote areas of the North Cascades. You'll visit two of Washington's most legendary wilderness hot springs, accessible only by hiking through old-growth forest and alpine terrain.

This itinerary is rated **expert** for a reason. You need to be an experienced backpacker with strong navigation skills, the right gear for variable mountain weather, and the physical fitness to cover 10+ miles with a full pack on steep, sometimes unmaintained trails.

## What Makes This Trip Special

- **Goldmyer Hot Springs**: a preserved wilderness hot spring with a rustic bathhouse, maintained by a non-profit stewardship organization
- **Gamma Hot Springs**: one of the most remote hot springs in the lower 48, accessible only via a strenuous off-trail scramble
- **Glacier Peak Wilderness**: pristine alpine terrain with views of glaciers, wildflower meadows, and old-growth forest
- **True wilderness experience**: no cell service, no crowds, no developed facilities

## Critical Safety Notes

> **Warning**: This itinerary involves significant backcountry risk. Do not attempt without:
>
> - Solid backpacking experience and fitness
> - Navigation skills (map, compass, GPS) — cell service is nonexistent
> - Bear canister or approved food storage (bears are active in this area)
> - Emergency communication device (Garmin inReach or similar) recommended
> - Current trail condition research — the Suiattle River Trail has washed out in recent years

### Goldmyer Hot Springs Access

Goldmyer is managed by the Northwest Wilderness Institute, a non-profit that limits access to preserve the wilderness experience. You **must** reserve in advance at [goldmyer.org](https://www.goldmyer.org). The property is caretaker-stewarded and has a capacity limit. Cost is approximately $20/person.

### Gamma Hot Springs Access

Gamma is the real challenge. The trailhead access involves a steep, eroded route that can be difficult to follow. The final approach to the springs involves scrambling over large boulders. **Do not attempt in wet conditions** — the rock is slick and falls are dangerous. The springs themselves are small and can be hot; test temperatures carefully before entering.

## Day-by-Day Highlights

### Goldmyer Hot Springs

The hike to Goldmyer follows the Middle Fork Snoqualmie River Trail for 4.5 miles through magnificent old-growth forest. The springs emerge from a hillside at about 145°F and are channeled through a series of cedar-plank pools that cool the water to a comfortable 105-110°F. A small bathhouse provides changing space, and a wood-fired sauna is available in cooler months.

The stewardship organization maintains a few rustic campsites near the springs. There is no potable water — treat or filter river water.

### Gamma Hot Springs

Gamma is accessed from the Suiattle River Trail, which connects to the Pacific Crest Trail. The route to the springs involves ~10 miles of hiking from the Suiattle trailhead, with significant elevation gain. The springs are in a spectacular alpine setting at the base of Gamma Peak, with small pools tucked among granite boulders.

> **Note**: The Suiattle River Road (Forest Road 26) has been impacted by washouts in recent years. Check current road and trail conditions at the [Darrington Ranger District](https://www.fs.usda.gov/recarea/mbs/recarea/?recid=17877) before planning your trip.

## Planning Notes

- **Reservations required** for Goldmyer: book at [goldmyer.org](https://www.goldmyer.org)
- **Trailhead parking**: Northwest Forest Pass required at both trailheads
- **Best window**: August through mid-September, when snow has melted and before fall rains begin
- **Water**: filter or treat all water; carry enough for the Gamma day hike
- **Wildlife**: bear canisters recommended; store all food and scented items properly
- **Leave no trace**: these are pristine wilderness areas — pack out everything, stay on trails, and respect the resource
